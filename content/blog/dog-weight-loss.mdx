# Dog Weight Loss Plan: Safe Calorie Cuts & Timeline

If your dog is overweight, reducing their daily calories is the most effective intervention available. But cutting calories too aggressively causes muscle loss, nutritional deficiencies, and metabolic slowdown that makes subsequent weight loss harder. This guide explains how to reduce calories safely and what realistic timeline to expect.

## Is My Dog Overweight?

Use the Body Condition Score (BCS) to assess your dog's weight before starting any diet plan:

| BCS Score | Description | Recommended Action |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| 1 to 3 | Underweight | Increase calories, consult vet |
| 4 to 5 | Ideal weight | Maintain current intake |
| 6 to 7 | Overweight | Reduce by 10 to 15 percent |
| 8 to 9 | Obese | Reduce by 20 to 25 percent, vet consult required |

To check BCS at home: run your hands along your dog's ribcage. You should feel each rib easily without pressing hard, but they should not be visibly prominent. Viewed from above, there should be a visible waist. Viewed from the side, there should be an abdominal tuck. If ribs are difficult to feel under fat and there is no visible waist, your dog is likely overweight.

## How Many Calories to Cut

For weight loss, veterinary guidelines recommend feeding at RER x 1.0 — the resting energy requirement with no activity multiplier added. This creates a moderate calorie deficit that promotes fat loss while preserving muscle mass.

Example: 25 kg overweight dog

Maintenance MER = 70 x (25)^0.75 x 1.6 = 1,120 kcal
Weight loss target = 70 x (25)^0.75 x 1.0 = 700 kcal
Daily reduction = 420 kcal (37.5 percent)

This is a significant reduction which is why progress must be monitored carefully and the plan adjusted if weight loss is too rapid or too slow.

## Safe Rate of Weight Loss

The safe rate of weight loss for dogs is 1 to 2 percent of current body weight per week. Faster than this risks muscle loss, vitamin deficiencies, and rebound weight gain once normal feeding resumes.

| Current Dog Weight | Safe Weekly Loss Target |
|-------------------|------------------------|
| 5 kg | 50 to 100g per week |
| 10 kg | 100 to 200g per week |
| 20 kg | 200 to 400g per week |
| 30 kg | 300 to 600g per week |
| 40 kg | 400 to 800g per week |

If your dog is losing weight faster than these targets, increase daily calories by 10 percent. If progress has stalled after 4 weeks, reduce by a further 10 percent and reassess.

## 12-Week Weight Loss Timeline

| Weeks | Expected Progress | Key Actions |
|-------|-----------------|-------------|
| 1 to 2 | Initial water weight loss, 1 to 2 percent drop | Establish routine, weigh weekly |
| 3 to 4 | True fat loss begins, energy may drop slightly | Stay consistent, no extra treats |
| 5 to 8 | Steady progress, visible body changes begin | Reassess BCS, adjust if needed |
| 9 to 12 | Approaching target, metabolism adapting | Begin gradual transition to maintenance |

## Practical Tips for Successful Dog Weight Loss

Measure all food portions on a kitchen scale for the first 2 weeks. This calibrates your eye for what the correct portion looks like and eliminates the variability of cup scooping.

Eliminate all high-calorie treats immediately. Replace biscuits, chews, and training treats with raw vegetables. Baby carrots at 4 kcal each, cucumber slices at 2 kcal, and green beans at 2 kcal per bean are excellent alternatives that most dogs enjoy.

Increase exercise very gradually. Add 5 minutes to daily walks every week. Exercise alone rarely produces meaningful weight loss in dogs, but it preserves muscle mass during calorie restriction and improves insulin sensitivity.

Feed multiple smaller meals. Splitting the daily calorie target into 3 meals keeps your dog feeling satisfied between feeds and reduces begging behaviour that leads to owners giving extra food.

Never give in to begging. Dogs are highly effective at soliciting food from humans. If you feed in response to begging, you will undermine the entire weight loss plan. Redirect begging behaviour with play or a stuffed toy.

Keep a simple log. Record your dog's weight every week and their daily food intake. Patterns become clear over 4 to 6 weeks and make it easy to know when to adjust.

## When to Involve Your Veterinarian

Always consult your vet before starting a weight loss plan if your dog is more than 20 percent above their ideal body weight. Severe obesity in dogs is a medical condition requiring supervised management.

Also consult your vet if your dog has joint disease, heart disease, diabetes, kidney disease, or is taking any medications. Some conditions require specific dietary modifications beyond simple calorie reduction.

If your dog fails to lose weight on a calorie-restricted diet despite strict compliance, ask your vet to test thyroid function. Hypothyroidism is a common cause of treatment-resistant obesity in dogs and is easily managed with daily medication once diagnosed.
