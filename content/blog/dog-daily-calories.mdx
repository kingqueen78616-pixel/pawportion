# How Many Calories Does a Dog Need Per Day? (2026)

Most dog owners ask this question every single day: **how much should I feed my dog?** The answer depends on your dog's weight, age, activity level, and reproductive status. This guide explains the exact formula vets use — and how to calculate it for your dog right now.

## The Short Answer

Most adult dogs need between **25 and 30 kcal per kg of body weight per day**. A 10 kg adult dog needs approximately 550–600 kcal daily. But this is just a starting point — your dog's exact needs depend on several factors we will cover below.

## Why Calorie Counting Matters for Dogs

Obesity is the number one nutritional problem in dogs worldwide. According to the Association for Pet Obesity Prevention, over 50% of dogs in the United States are overweight or obese. Overfeeding — even slightly — leads to serious long-term consequences:

- Joint problems and arthritis that worsen with every extra kilogram
- Diabetes and insulin resistance requiring lifelong medication
- Heart disease and significantly reduced lifespan
- Reduced energy, mobility, and quality of life
- Higher surgical risk and slower recovery from illness

The good news is that calorie management is simple once you know the right number. Most dogs reach their ideal weight within 3–6 months of accurate portion control alone — no special food required.

Knowing your dog's exact calorie needs is the single most important step you can take for their long-term health and happiness.

## The RER Formula — What Vets Use

Veterinarians calculate dog calorie needs using a two-step formula endorsed by the World Small Animal Veterinary Association (WSAVA):

### Step 1: Resting Energy Requirement (RER)

The RER is the number of calories your dog needs just to stay alive at rest — breathing, circulation, and basic organ function. It does not include any activity.

**RER = 70 x (body weight in kg)^0.75**

The exponent 0.75 is called the metabolic body weight. It accounts for the fact that larger animals have a lower metabolic rate per kilogram than smaller animals — a Great Dane does not need 10 times more calories than a Chihuahua even if it weighs 10 times more.

For a 10 kg dog: RER = 70 x (10)^0.75 = 70 x 5.62 = **394 kcal**

### Step 2: Maintenance Energy Requirement (MER)

The MER adds a multiplier based on your dog's life stage and activity level. This gives the actual daily calorie target your dog needs to maintain their current weight.

**MER = RER x Life Stage Factor**

| Life Stage or Status | MER Multiplier |
|----------------------|---------------|
| Neutered adult | RER x 1.6 |
| Intact adult | RER x 1.8 |
| Sedentary or inactive | RER x 1.2 |
| Weight loss prescribed | RER x 1.0 |
| Underweight, weight gain | RER x 1.7 |
| Puppy under 4 months | RER x 3.0 |
| Puppy 4 to 12 months | RER x 2.0 |
| Senior dog 7+ years | RER x 1.4 |
| Pregnant last trimester | RER x 3.0 |
| Nursing 1 to 4 puppies | RER x 4.0 |
| Nursing 5 or more puppies | RER x 8.0 |
| Light working dog | RER x 2.0 |
| Heavy working dog | RER x 5.0 |

### Full Example Calculation

**Dog:** Buddy, 10 kg, neutered adult, moderate activity, BCS 5/9

Step 1: RER = 70 x (10)^0.75 = 394 kcal
Step 2: MER = 394 x 1.6 = **630 kcal per day**

Buddy needs 630 kcal per day from all food sources — kibble and treats combined.

## Factors That Change Your Dog's Calorie Needs

### Age and Life Stage

Age is one of the most significant factors affecting calorie needs. Puppies need 2 to 3 times more calories per kilogram than adult dogs because they are growing rapidly. Their bodies are building muscle, bone, and organ tissue simultaneously.

Senior dogs aged 7 years and over need approximately 20% fewer calories than young adults. Their metabolism slows, they are typically less active, and they lose muscle mass more easily. The RER formula uses a 1.4 multiplier for seniors compared to 1.6 for neutered adults.

### Spay and Neuter Status

Spaying or neutering reduces a dog's calorie needs by approximately 15 to 20%. Sex hormones — particularly testosterone in males and oestrogen in females — influence metabolic rate. After neutering, the metabolic rate drops noticeably.

This is why neutered dogs use a 1.6 multiplier while intact dogs use 1.8. Without accounting for this difference, neutered dogs are routinely overfed and gradually gain weight.

### Activity Level

Activity level has a dramatic effect on calorie needs. A working dog such as a police dog, search and rescue dog, or competitive agility dog may need 3 to 5 times their RER — far more than the standard 1.6 multiplier suggests.

Conversely, a dog that spends most of the day sleeping may only need 1.2 times their RER. Many small breeds and brachycephalic breeds fall into this category.

When in doubt, start with the standard multiplier and adjust based on your dog's weight trend over 4 to 6 weeks.

### Body Condition Score

The Body Condition Score (BCS) is a 9-point scale used by vets to assess body fat. It is the most practical tool for adjusting calorie targets between vet visits.

- BCS 1 to 3: Underweight — increase calories, use 1.7 multiplier
- BCS 4 to 5: Ideal — maintain current intake
- BCS 6 to 7: Overweight — reduce by 10 to 15%
- BCS 8 to 9: Obese — reduce by 20 to 25%, consult vet

To check BCS at home: you should be able to feel your dog's ribs easily without pressing hard, but they should not be prominently visible. There should be a visible waist when viewed from above and an abdominal tuck when viewed from the side.

### Breed Characteristics

Some breeds have naturally higher or lower metabolisms regardless of size and activity level. Greyhounds, Border Collies, and Siberian Huskies tend to burn calories efficiently and may need more food than their weight suggests.

Basset Hounds, Bulldogs, Cocker Spaniels, and Labrador Retrievers are more prone to weight gain and often need the lower end of the calorie range. Labradors in particular have a genetic mutation affecting their appetite regulation, making them prone to obesity without careful portion control.

## How Many Cups of Food Per Day?

Once you know your dog's daily kcal target, the next step is converting that into cups or grams of food. This requires knowing the calorie density of your specific food.

**Cups per day = Daily kcal target divided by Kcal per cup**

Most dry kibble contains between 300 and 450 kcal per cup, but premium foods can reach 500 kcal per cup. Using an average figure instead of your food's actual value leads to significant over or underfeeding.

**Where to find kcal per cup:**
- On the bag under Calorie Content or Guaranteed Analysis
- On the manufacturer's website
- By calling the manufacturer's customer service line

**Example calculation:**

Buddy needs 630 kcal per day. His food contains 350 kcal per cup.
630 divided by 350 = 1.8 cups per day
Split into 2 meals: 0.9 cups in the morning and 0.9 cups in the evening.

## Do Not Forget Treats

Treats are the most commonly overlooked source of excess calories in dogs. Many owners feed the correct kibble portion but then add treats on top — effectively doubling or tripling calories without realising it.

A small commercial dog treat averages 5 to 25 kcal. A dental chew can contain 50 to 150 kcal. A piece of cheese or chicken used for training adds up quickly across a full day of sessions.

The general guideline is that treats should not exceed 10% of your dog's daily calorie budget. For a 630 kcal dog, that is 63 kcal maximum from treats — roughly 6 to 12 small treats depending on the brand.

Always deduct treat calories from your dog's daily kibble allowance. If your dog gets 50 kcal from treats, reduce kibble by the same amount.

## How to Monitor Progress

Accurate calorie calculation is a starting point — not a permanent fixed number. Individual dogs vary by 20 to 30% from calculated values due to genetics, gut microbiome, and breed-specific differences.

**Weigh your dog every 2 to 4 weeks.** Use the same scale at the same time of day. Track the trend over a full month rather than reacting to week-to-week fluctuations.

**Target weight trends:**
- Maintaining weight: calorie target is correct
- Gaining weight: reduce by 10% and reassess in 4 weeks
- Losing weight unintentionally: increase by 10% and monitor

For dogs on a prescribed weight loss plan, aim for 1 to 2% of body weight loss per week. Faster than this risks muscle loss.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many calories does a 10 kg dog need per day?

A 10 kg neutered adult dog with moderate activity needs approximately 560 to 630 kcal per day. The exact calculation is: RER = 70 x (10)^0.75 = 394 kcal, then MER = 394 x 1.6 = 630 kcal.

### How many calories does a puppy need per day?

Puppies under 4 months need 3 times their RER. A 5 kg puppy needs: RER = 70 x (5)^0.75 = 197 kcal, then MER = 197 x 3.0 = 591 kcal per day, split across 4 meals.

### Can I feed my dog too few calories?

Yes. Underfeeding causes muscle loss, immune dysfunction, nutrient deficiencies, and poor coat condition. Always aim for your dog's MER target — do not go below RER x 1.0 without veterinary supervision.

### How often should I feed my dog?

Most adult dogs do best with 2 meals per day — morning and evening. Puppies under 6 months need 3 to 4 meals per day. Large and giant breeds may benefit from 3 meals to reduce bloat risk.

### Does the type of food affect calorie needs?

No — your dog's calorie needs are the same regardless of food type. What changes is the portion size. A raw diet at 150 kcal per 100g requires a smaller portion than a kibble at 350 kcal per cup to deliver the same total calories.
