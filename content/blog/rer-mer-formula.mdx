# RER and MER Formula for Dogs — The Complete Guide

If you have ever wondered how vets calculate dog calorie needs, the answer is two formulas: RER and MER. This guide explains both formulas in plain language with worked examples for every life stage and activity level.

## What is RER?

RER stands for Resting Energy Requirement. It is the number of calories a dog needs just to stay alive at complete rest — supporting basic organ function, circulation, and breathing. Think of it as your dog's baseline calorie burn — what they would need even if they never moved from their bed.

The RER represents approximately 60 to 70 percent of a typical dog's total daily energy expenditure. Everything beyond RER accounts for activity, growth, reproduction, and thermoregulation.

## The RER Formula

RER = 70 x (body weight in kg)^0.75

The 0.75 exponent is called the metabolic body weight. It accounts for the fact that larger animals have a lower metabolic rate per kilogram of body weight than smaller animals. This is why a Great Dane does not need 30 times more calories than a Chihuahua even if it weighs 30 times more.

This formula was originally developed from research on mammalian energy metabolism and has been validated extensively in clinical veterinary settings. It is endorsed by the World Small Animal Veterinary Association (WSAVA) and taught in veterinary schools worldwide.

## What is MER?

MER stands for Maintenance Energy Requirement. It is the total number of calories a dog needs per day to maintain their current body weight including all physical activity, thermoregulation, and normal life functions.

MER is always higher than RER because it adds the energy cost of everything a dog does beyond basic survival. A working dog doing intense physical labour all day has a much higher MER than a sedentary house dog even if both have the same RER.

## The MER Formula

MER = RER x Life Stage Factor

The life stage factor is selected based on your dog's current status:

| Life Stage or Status | Factor | Clinical Rationale |
|---------------------|--------|------------------|
| Neutered adult | 1.6 | Standard recommendation post-neutering |
| Intact adult | 1.8 | Sex hormones increase metabolic rate |
| Sedentary adult | 1.2 | Inactive or obesity-prone breeds |
| Weight loss prescribed | 1.0 | Calorie restriction to RER level |
| Underweight, weight gain | 1.7 | Positive energy balance for recovery |
| Puppy under 4 months | 3.0 | Rapid early development phase |
| Puppy 4 to 12 months | 2.0 | Active growth, high protein turnover |
| Senior dog 7 plus years | 1.4 | Reduced activity and metabolism |
| Pregnant last trimester | 3.0 | Foetal growth and energy storage |
| Nursing 1 to 4 puppies | 4.0 | Milk production demands |
| Nursing 5 or more puppies | 8.0 | Extreme lactation energy demand |
| Light working dog | 2.0 | Sport, agility, or light duty work |
| Heavy working dog | 5.0 | Sled dogs, police dogs, herding dogs |

## Worked Examples for Every Life Stage

### Standard Neutered Adult

Bella: 12 kg, spayed female, moderate household activity

Step 1 - RER: 70 x (12)^0.75 = 70 x 6.35 = 445 kcal
Step 2 - Factor: Neutered adult = 1.6
Step 3 - MER: 445 x 1.6 = 712 kcal per day

Bella needs 712 kcal per day split between morning and evening meals.

### Growing Puppy

Max: 8 kg, intact male, 6 months old

Step 1 - RER: 70 x (8)^0.75 = 70 x 4.76 = 333 kcal
Step 2 - Factor: Puppy 4 to 12 months = 2.0
Step 3 - MER: 333 x 2.0 = 666 kcal per day

Max needs 666 kcal per day split across 3 meals.

### Senior Dog

Luna: 20 kg, spayed female, 9 years old, low activity

Step 1 - RER: 70 x (20)^0.75 = 70 x 9.46 = 662 kcal
Step 2 - Factor: Senior 7 plus = 1.4
Step 3 - MER: 662 x 1.4 = 927 kcal per day

Luna needs 927 kcal per day. This is significantly less than she needed as a young adult at 1.6, which would have been 1,059 kcal per day.

### Nursing Mother

Daisy: 25 kg, nursing 6 puppies

Step 1 - RER: 70 x (25)^0.75 = 70 x 11.18 = 783 kcal
Step 2 - Factor: Nursing 5 plus puppies = 8.0
Step 3 - MER: 783 x 8.0 = 6,264 kcal per day

Daisy needs over 6,000 kcal per day during peak lactation. This is why nursing dogs must have food available ad libitum and why they lose body condition so rapidly if underfed.

### Working Dog

Rex: 30 kg, police dog, full duty

Step 1 - RER: 70 x (30)^0.75 = 70 x 12.82 = 897 kcal
Step 2 - Factor: Heavy working dog = 5.0
Step 3 - MER: 897 x 5.0 = 4,485 kcal per day

Rex needs 4,485 kcal per day — more than four times what a neutered adult of the same weight would need.

## Why This Formula Matters

The RER and MER formula is the most accurate publicly available method for calculating individual dog calorie needs. Compared to bag feeding guidelines — which are generic and designed for an average intact adult dog — the formula accounts for your dog's actual neutering status, life stage, and activity level.

Using bag guidelines for a neutered, sedentary, senior dog will almost always lead to overfeeding and gradual weight gain. The formula corrects for all of these factors simultaneously.

## Limitations

No formula is perfect. Individual dogs can vary by 20 to 30 percent from the calculated MER due to genetics, gut microbiome differences, breed-specific metabolic rates, and health conditions. Hypothyroidism, for example, can reduce a dog's actual metabolic rate significantly below what the formula predicts.

Use the RER and MER formula as a calibrated starting point. Weigh your dog monthly and adjust portions by 10 percent up or down based on the weight trend over 4 to 6 weeks.
